UNCAVA
Design palette · v1 · dark-first

A quiet instrument panel with one indigo that earns attention.

UNCAVA is read for hours at a time by consultants scanning dense company and executive data. So the surface recedes: near-black grounds, hairline structure, no chrome that competes with a number. Colour is spent only where it carries meaning — an indigo for anything interactive, a copper for anything selected or linked, and five fixed status hues. Everything below is a token with a stated job. If a colour has no job, it is not in the palette.

Geist · UI JetBrains Mono · numerals Montserrat 200 · wordmark only Indigo #6E79F2 Copper #D4855C
01 · Surfaces

Depth comes from four steps and a hairline, not from shadow

On dark grounds a drop shadow reads as mud. Elevation is instead a step up the surface ladder plus a 1px border. Never skip a step — a popover on a card goes 0E1013 → 15181D, never straight to 1D2128.

Three text weights, all above 4.5:1

03 · Accents

Indigo means you can act on it. Copper means it is selected.

Indigo ships in two stops because one cannot do both jobs: the bright stop is for text, icons, strokes and chart lines on dark; the deep stop is the only one that may take a white label. Copper is the map's selection colour, the org-hierarchy connector, and the snap target — nothing else.

04 · Status semantics

Five fixed meanings, always tint plus saturated text

Status never appears as a solid block — a saturated fill in a dense table pulls the eye away from the data it is annotating. Tint the ground to 12%, keep the text at full strength, and skip the border unless the chip sits on a raised surface.

05 · Chart colour

Six categorical hues in a fixed order, one sequential ramp

Always assign the categorical ramp in order, so series 1 is indigo on every chart in the product and a reader learns the legend once. Past six categories, stop colouring and group the tail into "Other" — a seven-slice pie is a table wearing a costume. Quantities of one thing (revenue band, bubble size, heat) use the sequential ramp instead.

10 · Chart rules

Ink goes to data, not to furniture

Do
  • Baseline at rgba(255,255,255,.10); interior gridlines at .055 and horizontal only.
  • 1.75px strokes, round joins, one terminal dot on the latest point.
  • Area fills at 13% of the series hue, flat — never a gradient to transparent.
  • Axis labels and every figure in JetBrains Mono 10–11px at #7C8493.
  • Bars start at zero, 7px tall, 3px radius, on a #1D2128 track so the remainder is legible.
  • Give the reader the number as well as the shape — value on the right of each bar.
Don't
  • No gradient fills, drop shadows or 3D on any series.
  • No more than two series on one axis, and never two axes on one chart.
  • No pie beyond three slices; no donut without the total in the hole.
  • No colour that is not in the ramp, and never reorder it per chart.
  • No copper in a chart — it is reserved for selection and would read as a filter.
  • No animated redraw on data refresh; fade the new value in over 180ms.

12 · Light mode

Same roles, deeper hues

Light mode exists for print, exports and daylight offices. The bright indigo and mint that read well on near-black are too light on white, so each hue darkens while keeping its job. Elevation flips: on light, shadow does the work and the surface ladder flattens.
